# Project Odysseus — Business Model Canvas

**Purpose:** a working business-model canvas for strategy, partner discussions, and prioritisation.

**Core premise:** Project Odysseus delivers structured, confidence-building CAPS support for South African learners. Private tutoring is the current commercial engine; school, NGO, and community delivery extend reach through one safe, data-light platform.

| Block | Canvas |
|---|---|
| **Customer segments** | **Primary payers:** parents/guardians of Grade 8–12 learners seeking Mathematics support, especially families wanting visible progress and dependable tutoring. **Institutional payers:** schools, NGOs, and funders sponsoring learner cohorts. **Beneficiaries/users:** learners, parents/guardians, tutors, school/NGO coordinators, and community-outreach learners. Early focus: Grade 8–12 CAPS Mathematics; Grade 12 Paper 1 is the initial content priority. |
| **Value propositions** | For learners: personalised, confidence-building tutoring with a clear next step, CAPS-aligned practice, feedback, and progress visibility. For parents: trusted, safeguarded tutoring and clear released progress reports. For schools/NGOs: cohort delivery, coordinators' workflows, and aggregate, de-identified impact reporting. For constrained learners: low-data, mobile-first, text-first learning designed for shared low-end Android devices. Platform differentiators: structured progress tracking, an AI careers assistant (Odie), careers guidance, and a growing teacher-reviewed content and diagnostic library. |
| **Channels** | Direct referrals and local parent networks; the public website and enquiry/WhatsApp path; tutor and parent word of mouth; school partnerships and coordinator-led rollout; NGO/funder partnerships; community venues such as churches, mosques, and community centres. Learners and families are served through role-based web dashboards and tutoring sessions/classes. |
| **Customer relationships** | High-trust, human-led tutoring supported by digital continuity between sessions. Parent/guardian permission and safeguarding are built into learner contact. Learners receive focused next-step guidance rather than an overwhelming dashboard; parents receive enabled reports; coordinators receive scoped cohort insights. Retention is supported by progress evidence, regular tutor feedback, and relevant learning/careers support. |
| **Revenue streams** | **Current:** paid private 1:1 and small-group tutoring, funded by parents/guardians. **Planned commercial expansion:** school class contracts; school or parent-funded programmes; NGO/funder-sponsored cohorts; potentially paid institutional reporting or programme-management packages where it adds clear value. **Mission delivery:** community outreach is free to learners and supported by surplus from paid strands, partners, grants, or sponsorships. Pricing, packages, and unit economics remain to be validated; this canvas intentionally does not invent fee levels. |
| **Key resources** | Trusted tutors and reviewers; CAPS Grade 8–12 Mathematics curriculum map; teacher-reviewed learning content and proprietary practice/diagnostic item bank; the React + Supabase platform; secure role-based data, reporting, and private file storage; Project Odysseus brand and local relationships; learner-progress and impact data managed with POPIA safeguards. |
| **Key activities** | Deliver and schedule quality tutoring; recruit, vet, train, and support tutors/volunteers; build and teacher-review CAPS content; develop diagnostics and progress interventions; maintain a secure, data-light platform; onboard and support families and partner organisations; manage safeguarding, consent, POPIA, reporting, payments, and tutor payroll; measure learner progress and programme impact. |
| **Key partners** | Parents/guardians; schools and their coordinators; NGOs and community organisations; funders/sponsors; community venues; qualified teachers and senior tutors for content review; vetted tutors and volunteers; technology providers (Supabase and AI infrastructure); potential future safeguarding/background-check providers. |
| **Cost structure** | Tutor compensation, payroll, recruitment, vetting, and training; content authoring, teacher review, and quality assurance; platform hosting, storage, AI usage, monitoring, and backup; safeguarding and POPIA compliance; partner onboarding and customer support; marketing/referral activity; administration, finance, and reporting; data-light/offline product development; community-delivery support and volunteer coordination. |

## Operating logic

```text
Paid private tutoring + school / NGO contracts
                ↓
High-quality tutors + secure, data-light CAPS platform
                ↓
Better learner confidence, learning continuity, and visible progress
                ↓
Parent retention + institutional renewal + funder-ready aggregate impact evidence
                ↓
Sustainable expansion of subsidised NGO and free community delivery
```

## Critical assumptions to test

1. Parents will pay for tutoring that combines human support with measurable progress and platform value-adds.
2. School/NGO coordinators value safe cohort management and aggregate impact reporting enough to adopt or fund the programme.
3. Tutor quality, safeguarding, and low-data accessibility will produce stronger retention and referrals than tutoring alone.
4. The economics of paid delivery can cover core platform costs and create a reliable subsidy for community outreach.
5. The teacher-review gate and organisation-level data isolation stay non-negotiable as delivery scales.

## Success measures

- Learner retention, attendance, assignment completion, confidence pulse, and improvement against learner goals.
- Parent satisfaction, referrals, and package renewal.
- Tutor capacity, quality, and safeguarding/vetting compliance.
- School/NGO cohort retention, contract renewal, and de-identified learner outcomes.
- Cost to acquire a paying family or institution, tutor utilisation, gross margin by delivery strand, and subsidy generated for community delivery.
- Data-light performance and safe-operation measures: mobile load weight, platform reliability, zero material privacy/safeguarding incidents, and completed access-control checks.

## Strategic guardrails

- Build first for the learner with the least reliable device, data, and connectivity.
- Do not compromise child safety, parent/guardian consent, POPIA, or cross-organisation data isolation for growth.
- Only publish learner-facing content after qualified-teacher review.
- Keep the learner experience confidence-building and focused, rather than judgemental or cognitively overloaded.
