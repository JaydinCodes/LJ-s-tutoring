# Grade 12 Mathematics textbook taxonomy

Source: *Everything Maths Grade 12 Mathematics, Version 1 CAPS*, supplied as
`Gr12_Mathematics_Learner_Eng.pdf`. The implementation maps all ten textbook
chapters into 73 atomic skills and 73 question families. These records are
intentionally `teacher_review`, not learner-facing or recommendation-eligible.

ATP alignment source: `1.600 ATP 2023-24 Gr 12 Maths final (1).pdf`.

The 2023/24 Grade 12 Mathematics ATP adds 16 assessment-specific families,
bringing the current map to 89 skills/question families. They include simple
and compound depreciation, logarithm-base graph behaviour, sine/cosine/area
rules in 2D/3D, line inclination, motion/rates, named similarity proofs,
statistical displays/skewness, three-event Venn diagrams and contingency
tables.

## Coverage

| Textbook strand | Atomic skills | Examples of distinct question families |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Sequences and series | 13 | arithmetic term/index, quadratic differences, geometric ratio, finite/infinite sums |
| Functions | 14 | domain/range, inverse restrictions, average gradient, quadratic features, exponential/log graphs and equations |
| Finance | 7 | simple/compound growth or depreciation, period via logs, annuities, option comparison |
| Trigonometry | 8 | identity proof, compound/double angles, sine/cosine/area rules, 2D/3D modelling |
| Polynomials | 6 | polynomial division, remainder/factor theorem, cubic roots and factorisation |
| Differential calculus | 9 | numerical/algebraic limits, first principles, tangents, graph sketching, optimisation and motion/rates |
| Analytical geometry | 6 | line tools, inclination, line relationships, circle equations/intersections/tangents |
| Euclidean geometry | 9 | theorem justification, named similarity proofs, ratios, polygons and Pythagoras |
| Statistics | 6 | displays, skewness/outliers, summary statistics, regression and correlation |
| Probability | 11 | identities, two/three-event Venn diagrams, trees, contingency tables, dependence and counting |

## ATP sequence and assessment use

The learning system may use the ATP term to pace what is offered, but never to
assume a learner has mastered a topic because the calendar has moved on:

| Term | ATP strands | System use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sequences, functions, trigonometry | Build diagnostic and practice pools; prepare the Term 1 SBA evidence map. |
| 2 | Euclidean/analytical geometry, polynomials and calculus | Track proof, diagram and modelling forms independently; prepare June assessment evidence. |
| 3 | Finance, statistics, counting and probability | Track contextual interpretation separately from calculation; prepare trial evidence. |
| 4 | Revision and final examination | Use released evidence to create mixed, exam-style practice aligned to Paper 1 and Paper 2 distribution. |

## How a mark is recorded

Every evidence point must name both a skill and, where applicable, its question
family. For example, a learner can be recorded separately for:

```text
Probability -> Tools and techniques -> Tree diagrams
Probability -> Tools and techniques -> Dependence of events
Probability -> Identities -> Inclusion-exclusion / Venn diagrams
```

The same applies to every strand: representation (`diagram`, `graph`, `word
problem`, `symbolic`), and cognitive demand (`procedure`, `application`,
`reasoning`) are stored with each question family. Scores are never collapsed
into an undifferentiated "Probability" or "Algebra" mark.

## Release process

1. A Grade 12 Mathematics educator reviews and approves the taxonomy wording.
2. Content authors create diagnostics, reteach, guided, mixed-practice and
   extension activities for each question family.
3. An educator approves the activity, answer/rubric and accessibility notes.
4. Tutors record question-level evidence and approve any learner-facing next
   action.

No source content is automatically published from the textbook. That preserves
the tutor-review safeguard and avoids treating a textbook outline as an approved
teaching sequence.
