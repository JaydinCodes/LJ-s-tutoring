# Grade 9 Term 1 distractor-feedback semantic review checklist

This checklist is intentionally a human academic review aid. The QA script validates stable mapping, completeness, uniqueness, option integrity and prohibited generic fallbacks; it does not certify that wording diagnoses every misconception correctly.

For each row, confirm that the feedback identifies the misconception represented by the selected answer, states a correct next mathematical idea, remains grade appropriate, and does not reveal the complete solution too early.

## Q.G9.S4.PRACTICE.REAL.01

- Prompt: Which number is irrational?
- Correct answer: √20
- Worked solution: 20 is not a perfect square, so √20 cannot be written as a ratio of integers. The other choices are rational.
- B. 0.25 — 0.25 is rational because it equals 1/4. A terminating decimal can be written as a fraction of integers.
- C. −7 — −7 is rational because it equals −7/1. A negative sign does not make a number irrational.
- D. 3/5 — 3/5 is already a ratio of two integers, so it is rational. An irrational number cannot be expressed in that form.

## Q.G9.S4.PRACTICE.REAL.02

- Correct answer: A rational integer
- Worked solution: √121 = 11. It is a natural number, an integer and therefore rational.

## Q.G9.S4.PRACTICE.HCF-LCM.04

- Prompt: A learner says the HCF of 16 and 24 is 384. What is the correction?
- Correct answer: HCF is 8, the greatest factor common to both
- Worked solution: 16 = 2⁴ and 24 = 2³ × 3. The common part is 2³ = 8.
- A. HCF is 384, because numbers are multiplied — 384 is the product of 16 and 24, not a shared divisor. HCF means the greatest number that divides both exactly.
- B. HCF is 16, because it is the smaller number — The smaller number is not automatically a common factor: 24 is not divisible by 16. Check which factors both numbers share.
- D. HCF is 40, because factors are added — Adding 16 and 24 gives 40, but a common factor must divide both numbers. Test divisors such as 8 instead.

## Q.G9.S4.PRACTICE.RATIO-RATE.01

## Q.G9.S4.PRACTICE.PROPORTION.03
