# Learner Journey

## Actual journey

1. **Sign in** — password or “magic link”; role redirects to the student dashboard.
2. **Orient** — dashboard combines a greeting, “Needs attention”, battle plan, results/mastery context and supporting modules.
3. **Choose work** — assignments use a priority queue; learning recommendations deep-link to diagnostic or practice activities.
4. **Complete work** — learner enters a typed/selected answer and confidence rating; submission is evaluated server-side.
5. **Understand feedback** — correct answers receive confirmation; configured misconceptions receive an explanation, guidance and retry.
6. **Finish** — activity completion confirms evidence was saved and links to progress.
7. **Review progress** — subject/topic mastery is shown separately from released school results.
8. **Receive support** — tutor sessions, reports, notifications and careers are separate destinations.

## Main points of confusion

- “Magic link” is not framed as password recovery.
- A first-time learner must interpret several dashboard surfaces before knowing which one has priority.
- Mastery evidence and school marks are responsibly separated, but the learner is not clearly taught why the values may differ.
- Confidence collection is educationally valuable, but its role should be explained once and then kept lightweight.
- Completing practice does not clearly reveal when or how understanding will be checked again.
- Internal educational concepts occasionally leak into learner language instead of being translated into an action.

## Recommended coherent experience

### 1. Today

Lead with one primary instruction:

> Work on Difference of Two Squares for 8 minutes. Two recent answers suggest you may be confusing `(a-b)^2` with `(a-b)(a+b)`.

Below it, show urgent assignments and only then secondary progress/results context.

### 2. Activity

Keep the mathematical prompt visually dominant. Ask confidence near submission, explain why it is collected, and preserve the answer during retry or network ambiguity.

### 3. Feedback

Use a consistent structure:

- What happened.
- Why learners commonly make this mistake.
- One worked contrast or hint.
- The next action.

Never expose rule codes or imply a diagnosed misconception from one uncertain response.

### 4. Completion

Replace generic completion with evidence:

> You corrected the square-of-a-difference error on your second attempt. We will check this skill again in 7 days before calling it secure.

### 5. Progress

Use four plain-language stages mapped to system states:

- Starting — not enough independent evidence yet.
- Building — some correct work, still inconsistent or assisted.
- Secure — repeated independent success.
- Retained — success again after time has passed.

Show school results in a separate section labelled “Marks from submitted work”.

### 6. Human support

Tutor and guardian summaries should repeat the same evidence chain: observed error, intervention completed, later check, and current confidence—not only percentages.
